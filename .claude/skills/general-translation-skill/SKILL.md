# Natural Cultural Localization

## Purpose

Apply this Skill across Seung Park's website and YouTube projects. Produce content that feels native, current, conversational, persuasive, and culturally appropriate while protecting all material facts.

Do not treat localization as word substitution. Recreate the intended meaning, emotional effect, business purpose, and desired audience action in the target language.

Project-, brand-, and audience-specific instructions may refine this Skill. They may not weaken its factual safeguards.

## Core rule

> Creative and promotional language is culturally rewritten for equivalent effect. Accuracy-critical language is naturally translated with its facts, qualifications, rights, duties, risks, and limitations unchanged.

## Classify before writing

Classify every passage, field, subtitle, voiceover line, label, and metadata item as one of three modes.

### Mode A — Creative localization

Use for:

- website headlines, hooks, slogans, introductions, service descriptions, benefits, navigation, buttons, and calls to action;
- advertisements, brochures, emails, social posts, campaign copy, and customer-facing explanations;
- YouTube titles, thumbnails, openings, scripts, transitions, descriptions, captions, and voiceovers;
- brand storytelling, educational storytelling, and conversational customer communication.

Rules:

1. Preserve the source's business purpose, core meaning, brand position, and intended response.
2. Rewrite naturally for the target locale; do not preserve source syntax merely to appear faithful.
3. Adapt formality, honorifics, warmth, directness, rhythm, sentence length, humor, idioms, emotional intensity, examples, and persuasion style.
4. Reflect the audience's industry knowledge, cultural expectations, likely concerns, and decision stage.
5. Use familiar everyday language unless the audience expects professional terminology.
6. Create interest, intrigue, curiosity, and momentum without clickbait or manipulation.
7. Make the result sound as though a strong native copywriter or creator wrote it first in that language.
8. Prefer a clear benefit and next step over decorative language.

### Mode B — Strict factual translation

Use for:

- legal, contract, regulatory, compliance, and licensing content;
- financial, accounting, tax, investment, lending, and insurance content;
- pricing, fees, billing, payments, refunds, cancellation, and service limits;
- security, privacy, consent, recording, data retention, and accessibility statements;
- credentials, statistics, technical specifications, disclaimers, eligibility, dates, and deadlines;
- any statement whose alteration could change a person's rights, duties, cost, risk, or decision.

Rules:

1. Preserve every fact, amount, unit, date, name, defined term, qualification, exception, condition, obligation, right, warning, limitation, and uncertainty.
2. Improve naturalness and readability without changing scope or legal and commercial effect.
3. Do not add hooks, stronger promises, softened warnings, emotional pressure, or broader claims.
4. Never invent advice, authority, credentials, savings, performance, statistics, guarantees, or conclusions.
5. Keep approved terminology consistent across pages, captions, interfaces, and documents.
6. Flag material ambiguity instead of guessing.
7. Recommend qualified human review when legal, tax, accounting, financial, regulatory, or contractual consequences are material.

### Mode C — Mixed content

Use when promotional and accuracy-critical language appear together.

1. Separate the creative layer from protected facts.
2. Localize the creative layer under Mode A.
3. Translate protected facts under Mode B.
4. Recombine them clearly.
5. Confirm that the hook does not hide, contradict, exaggerate, or weaken a material condition.

When a single sentence creates avoidable risk, split it into a persuasive headline and a precise factual explanation.

## Protected strings

Do not translate, rewrite, normalize, or autocorrect the following unless Seung explicitly requests it:

- brand names, personal names, legal entity names, product names, and approved trademarks;
- URLs, email addresses, phone numbers, usernames, account identifiers, and social handles;
- passwords, verification codes, API keys, tokens, and credentials;
- filenames, file paths, commands, source code, variables, translation keys, IDs, and database fields;
- quoted text identified as requiring exact reproduction;
- approved prices, quantities, percentages, dates, version numbers, and plan names.

Preserve placeholders such as `{name}`, `{{price}}`, `%s`, `${total}`, and HTML/Markdown syntax exactly.

## Required context analysis

Before drafting, determine as many of these as the task provides:

- source language and authoritative source;
- target language and locale, such as `en-US`, `ko-KR`, `es-MX`, or `zh-TW`;
- target culture and geographic market;
- audience, role, age range, language fluency, and knowledge level;
- industry and transaction context;
- purpose: inform, persuade, reassure, convert, entertain, explain, or retain attention;
- channel: website, landing page, interface, YouTube, caption, voiceover, ad, email, or social media;
- placement and length constraint;
- brand voice, formality, emotional tone, and desired action;
- facts, terminology, and claims that must remain protected.

If context is missing but the task remains safely answerable, use the most likely market convention and state a brief assumption only when it materially affects the result. Ask one concise question only when different interpretations would substantially change the outcome.

## Localization workflow

1. Identify the authoritative source and extract its non-negotiable meaning.
2. Mark protected strings and accuracy-critical claims.
3. Divide the content into Mode A, B, or C units.
4. Determine the target audience, culture, industry, situation, channel, and desired action.
5. Draft directly from the authoritative source into the target language; avoid translating through an intermediate language when possible.
6. For Mode A, recreate equivalent cultural and emotional effect instead of sentence-by-sentence similarity.
7. For Mode B, preserve meaning and scope precisely while using natural target-language grammar.
8. Reassemble mixed content without allowing promotional language to distort protected facts.
9. Read the output as a native audience member and remove translation-like phrasing.
10. Run the factual, cultural, channel, and technical QA checks below.

## Cultural adaptation standards

Adapt deliberately rather than stereotypically.

- Match local norms for directness, politeness, hierarchy, honorifics, trust, proof, urgency, and calls to action.
- Replace an idiom only with a culturally equivalent expression that preserves intent.
- Use local everyday phrasing and market terminology when accurate.
- Avoid jokes, metaphors, gestures, colors, symbols, comparisons, and urgency devices that may be confusing or inappropriate in the target culture.
- Do not imitate accents, caricature speech, or rely on cultural stereotypes.
- Do not assume all speakers of a language share one culture; honor the requested locale.
- Preserve intentional bilingual brand language when it strengthens recognition or clarity.
- Prefer respectful and inclusive language appropriate to the audience and platform.

## Persuasive writing controls

For promotional content:

- lead with the customer benefit or tension that matters most;
- connect features to practical outcomes;
- use curiosity gaps only when the content honestly resolves them;
- prefer credible specificity over superlatives;
- keep the promise proportional to available evidence;
- end with a clear, culturally appropriate next step;
- never manufacture scarcity, deadlines, testimonials, popularity, savings, or performance data;
- never turn a possibility into a certainty.

Examples:

- Unsafe: `This AI will save every business $100,000 a year.`
- Safer creative version: `Give your team more time to serve customers—and reduce the routine work that drives operating costs.`
- Strict supporting fact: `Actual savings vary by staffing, wages, usage, configuration, and existing systems.`

## Website standards

When localizing websites:

1. Localize the complete user journey, not only visible paragraphs.
2. Cover headings, body copy, navigation, buttons, forms, field labels, validation, errors, confirmations, tooltips, chatbot messages, consent text, accessibility labels, metadata, structured data, and image alt text.
3. Keep headings scannable and CTAs action-oriented.
4. Preserve the established brand voice across all pages.
5. Use locale-aware date, time, number, currency, address, and telephone formats, but never change the underlying value without authorization.
6. Do not translate URLs, slugs, route names, localization keys, or code identifiers unless the implementation plan explicitly calls for localized routes.
7. Check text expansion, wrapping, mobile layout, buttons, forms, and navigation in every target language.
8. Ensure language switching is visible, user-controlled, persistent when appropriate, and does not overwrite user-entered content.
9. Keep translations complete. Do not silently fall back to another language for legal, pricing, consent, or transactional content.
10. Protect SEO intent while making titles and descriptions sound natural; do not keyword-stuff.

## YouTube standards

When localizing YouTube content:

1. Adapt the title, thumbnail text, opening hook, spoken script, on-screen text, description, chapters, captions, and CTA as one coordinated package.
2. Make the opening immediately understandable to the target audience.
3. Prioritize spoken naturalness: breathing, rhythm, emphasis, sentence length, and pronunciation.
4. Keep thumbnail text short, legible, and complementary to the title rather than repetitive.
5. Preserve the video's actual promise; do not use a title or thumbnail the video cannot satisfy.
6. Rework examples and analogies for the target market when doing so does not alter facts.
7. Match the platform's conversational style without using slang that feels forced or quickly dated.
8. Ensure captions match the approved spoken meaning and retain names, numbers, qualifications, and warnings.
9. For dubbed or voiceover content, avoid unnatural literal sentence order and fit the timing without deleting material facts.
10. Preserve citations, disclosures, sponsorship statements, and regulated claims accurately.

## Dialogue and customer communication

When localizing conversations, AI receptionist messages, chat, voice, or video scripts:

- identify who is speaking to whom, their relationship, and the service stage;
- match the appropriate level of politeness and warmth;
- preserve turn-taking intent, questions, confirmations, and escalation conditions;
- favor short, speakable sentences;
- allow the customer to change languages explicitly even when automatic detection is used;
- confirm critical details such as names, dates, times, addresses, order items, quantities, and prices;
- avoid translating uncertain audio or ambiguous customer intent as fact;
- route sensitive, exceptional, disputed, or out-of-scope matters to a person under the project's escalation rules.

## Language-specific baseline

These are defaults, not substitutes for locale research.

### English for the United States

- Use polished, direct, benefit-led business English.
- Prefer short sentences and active voice.
- Avoid exaggerated claims and excessive formality.
- Use U.S. spelling, `$` currency format, and locally familiar terms when applicable.

### Korean for South Korea and Korean-American audiences

- Use concise, respectful, persuasive language with natural formal endings such as `입니다`, `합니다`, and `드립니다` when appropriate.
- Avoid excessive subject repetition and translation-like noun strings.
- Adjust honorifics and directness to the relationship and setting.
- For Korean-American audiences, retain familiar English business terms only when they improve understanding.

### Spanish

- Identify the target locale; do not assume one Spanish register fits every market.
- Choose `tú`, `usted`, or plural forms consistently according to audience, brand, and region.
- Prefer warm, natural phrasing without unnecessary formality or literal English constructions.

### Chinese

- Confirm Simplified or Traditional Chinese and the target market.
- Use the market's natural terminology and punctuation.
- Do not mix Mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, or diaspora conventions without a reason.

### Japanese

- Match politeness to the brand and customer relationship.
- Prefer concise, natural Japanese over direct English sentence structure.
- Avoid overclaiming; build trust through clarity, reassurance, and appropriate specificity.

### Russian and Indonesian

- Confirm locale and audience when material.
- Use natural contemporary business language and culturally appropriate levels of formality.
- Avoid translating English sales idioms literally.

For any language not listed, apply the same core workflow and locale-specific judgment.

## Technical implementation safeguards

When editing code or content files:

1. Inspect the project's existing localization structure and naming conventions before changing anything.
2. Preserve keys, interpolation variables, markup, escape sequences, and component behavior.
3. Add missing locale entries without deleting or renaming approved keys unless Seung explicitly approves it.
4. Keep one authoritative source per approved string and avoid competing duplicates.
5. Use UTF-8 and verify punctuation, quotation marks, apostrophes, line breaks, and right-to-left behavior when applicable.
6. Do not expose secrets or translate confidential identifiers.
7. Verify locale fallback behavior, pluralization, gender, truncation, sorting, search, and input validation where relevant.
8. Inspect diffs after edits. Expected removal of approved content is zero unless expressly authorized.

## Quality assurance checklist

Do not report completion until the applicable checks pass.

### Meaning and factual integrity

- The business purpose and intended audience response are preserved.
- All protected facts, qualifications, conditions, numbers, names, and warnings remain accurate.
- No new claim, guarantee, credential, statistic, savings figure, or conclusion was invented.
- Promotional language does not overpower a material disclosure.

### Native quality and culture

- The copy sounds originally written in the target language.
- Tone, honorifics, directness, examples, idioms, and CTA fit the locale and audience.
- The wording avoids stereotypes, awkward literal phrasing, and forced slang.
- The hook is interesting but honest and supported.

### Channel fitness

- Website content is scannable, consistent, accessible, and conversion-aware.
- YouTube language is speakable, paced well, and aligned across title, thumbnail, hook, script, and CTA.
- Conversation text sounds natural aloud and preserves confirmation and escalation logic.

### Technical integrity

- URLs, email addresses, code, keys, placeholders, filenames, paths, and IDs are unchanged.
- Locale files are complete and syntactically valid.
- Text fits desktop and mobile layouts.
- Dates, times, currencies, and numbers display correctly without changing their values.
- No approved content was deleted, reduced, renamed, moved, or replaced without approval.

## Output behavior

Unless Seung requests another format:

1. Give the strongest recommended localized version first.
2. Provide the final target-language copy ready to use.
3. Add alternatives only when they offer meaningfully different positioning.
4. Briefly identify any material assumption, protected fact, or human-review requirement.
5. Do not clutter the response with literal back-translation unless it helps approval or Seung requests it.
6. If multiple languages are requested, keep terminology, brand promise, and factual claims aligned while allowing each language to sound culturally native.

## Definition of done

The work is complete only when the target content:

- reads naturally to the intended local audience;
- fits the industry, situation, tone, platform, and placement;
- creates honest interest or curiosity where appropriate;
- preserves all accuracy-critical meaning;
- protects strings and implementation syntax;
- passes channel and layout checks; and
- contains no unauthorized deletion or unsupported claim.
